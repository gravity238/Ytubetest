What Did Ancient Humans Do in the Middle of the Night?

---

You wake at 3am and assume something is wrong with you. It isn't.

For most of human history, the middle of the night was not a malfunction — it was a scheduled, named hour that everyone shared. People slept in two shifts. Between them, they got up. They prayed, talked, mended, brewed, checked the animals, fed the fire, or walked to a neighbour's house. That waking interval had a name: THE WATCH.

This video drops you into a cave at full dark and walks you through a night the way your ancestors actually lived it — the fire, the debt of the shift, the first sleep, the watch, the second sleep, and the light bulb that quietly deleted all of it.

We also look at the honest complications. Historian A. Roger Ekirch pulled over 500 references out of the archive, and Thomas Wehr's lab reproduced biphasic sleep in the 1990s — but modern studies of the Hadza, San and Tsimane do not show two-shift sleep, and Niall Boyce has challenged how universal the pattern really was. What survives all of it is a quieter conclusion: the eight-hour block isn't a law of nature either.

You are the only animal that ever stopped hiding from the dark.
